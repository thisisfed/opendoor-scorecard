Opendoor is rated on nine pillars, from growth and unit economics to dilution, valuation and the housing market. Each metric earns 0 to 10 points on a straight line between two published levels, and a pillar is the average of its metrics. The overall score is the weighted average of the pillars (Growth, Unit economics, Profitability, Execution and Balance sheet 15% each; Dilution 10%; Valuation, Momentum and Housing market 5% each), and the grade follows from it: A from 8.0, B from 6.5, C from 5.0, D from 3.5, F below. It is an independent summary of public numbers, not a forecast, a recommendation or investment advice.

Debt, split by what backs it

Most of the debt is secured by the homes themselves

5.9

Balance sheet

Home-backed debt vs the homes it funds92.1%
Debt $2,364MCushion $204MHomes held $2,568M

Opendoor holds homes on its books at $2,568M against $2,364M of debt secured on them, so $204M (7.9%) of its own money is in them. That is the cushion before a fall in home prices reaches the lenders. Lenders can take the homes but, except in limited cases, cannot claim the company.

Method. Each metric scores 0–10 on a straight line between a "0 points" and a "10 points" value (all of them are listed above). Pillars average their metrics, and the overall score weights the pillars as shown. Grades: A 8+, B 6.5+, C 5+, D 3.5+, F below. The colour at the top of the page follows the score, from red to green. Debt is split by what backs it: home-backed debt, which lenders can recover only from the homes, is measured against the homes it funds, and the company's own convertible notes against its cash. Potential new shares count only once they are live at today's price (see Details). A quarterly figure one quarter old is labelled; older than that, it stops counting. The thresholds and weights are judgement calls and have not been back-tested against share-price returns.
