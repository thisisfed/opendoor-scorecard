Debt, split by what backs it

Most of the debt is secured by the homes themselves

6.7

Balance sheet

Home-backed debt vs the homes it funds95.5%
Debt $1,762MCushion $83MHomes held $1,845M

Opendoor holds homes on its books at $1,845M against $1,762M of debt secured on them, so $83M (4.5%) of its own money is in them. That is the cushion before a fall in home prices reaches the lenders. Lenders can take the homes but, except in limited cases, cannot claim the company.

Method. Each metric scores 0–10 on a straight line between a "0 points" and a "10 points" value (all of them are listed above). Pillars average their metrics, and the overall score weights the pillars as shown. Grades: A 8+, B 6.5+, C 5+, D 3.5+, F below. The colour at the top of the page follows the score, from red to green. Debt is split by what backs it: home-backed debt, which lenders can recover only from the homes, is measured against the homes it funds, and the company's own convertible notes against its cash. Potential new shares count only once they are live at today's price (see Details). A quarterly figure one quarter old is labelled; older than that, it stops counting. The thresholds and weights are judgement calls and have not been back-tested against share-price returns. Q3 2026 revenue is estimated at $1,027M (+12.2% YoY) until Opendoor reports: 2,728 homes sold (Opendoor's tracker) x $376K, the average revenue per home in Q1 2026 and Q2 2026. Homes sold are +6.2% on a year earlier, so the estimate assumes revenue per home +5.6% above the $356K of a year earlier. Opendoor guided revenue up at least 20% ($1,098M or more), which implies a higher revenue per home than this estimate assumes. Cash, notes, book value and share count include: $135M of 2026 convertible notes repaid at maturity; $650M 0% convertible notes due 2030, $158M buyback of 45.3M shares, $52.5M capped call.
